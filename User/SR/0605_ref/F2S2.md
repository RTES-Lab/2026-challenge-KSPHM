## 1. Introduction

Traditional reliability methods are based on evaluating the lifetime distribution of a population of engineering systems by statistically analyzing a sample of time-to-failure data [1]. Different units of the same population generally have different material properties, manufacturing qualities, and operating conditions. These differences often lead to tremendous variations in estimating the remaining lifetimes of engineering systems. Degradation signals, such as vibration signals, temperature and pressure, often exhibit increasing or decreasing trends that are typically correlated with underlying degradation processes leading up to failure. Prognostic degradation modeling as a field of study that focuses on how to utilize degradation signals to predict the remaining useful life (RUL) of a system has been researched in the literature [2–4]. With the increasing growth of Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT), prognostic degradation modeling has become a focal point of many recent research efforts—prognostics is one of the core components of the modern term predictive analytics [5–7]. Prognostic degradation models leverage the correlation between degradation data and the progression of physical degradation and damage accumulation. However, operating conditions,

## ABSTRACT

The growth of the Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) has generated a renewed emphasis on research of prognostic degradation modeling whereby degradation signals, such as vibration signals, temperature and acoustic emissions, are used to estimate the state-of-health and predict the remaining useful life (RUL). Besides the inherent system state, external operating conditions, such as the rotational speed and load also play a significant role in the behavior of degradation signals. Time-varying operating conditions often cause two major effects on the degradation signals. First, they change the degradation rate of systems. Second, they cause signal jumps at condition change-points. These two factors make RUL prediction more difficult under time-varying operating conditions. This paper proposes a RUL prediction method by introducing these two factors into a state-space model. Changes in the degradation rate are introduced into a state transition function, and jumps in the degradation signals are introduced into a measurement function. The separate analysis of these two factors makes it possible to distinguish their own contributions to RUL prediction, thus avoiding false alarms and improving the prediction accuracy. The effectiveness of the proposed method is demonstrated using both a simulation study and an accelerated degradation test of rolling element bearings.

For example, the vibration level and degradation rate of rotating components, such as gears, bearings and pumps, tend to increase with rotational speeds. Yet the majority of the degradation modeling literature assumed that the prevailing operating conditions of a system remain constant for the entire prediction timeline.

Some limited literature has considered the impact of time-varying operating conditions on prognostic degradation modeling. These papers can be roughly classified into two categories. The first class focuses on guiding stepstress accelerated degradation tests for reliability assessment of a system population [8–11]. The focus of this class pertains to reliability testing with little or no emphasis on prognostics. The second class assumes that the effects of time-varying operating conditions manifest themselves in the behavior of degradation signals, and focuses on predicting RUL of a system operating under time-varying conditions based on the available degradation signals. Since this paper focuses on the topic of RUL prediction, we provide some examples that belong to the second class mentioned earlier.

The major research gap in current literature is that they did not distinguish the different effects of time-varying operating conditions on the underlying degradation process of the system state and the dynamic behavior of degradation signals. To fulfill this research gap, this paper distinguishes the effects of time-varying operating conditions into two different parts. The first part happens on the underlying system state, i.e., the degradation rate of the system state will change along with the time-varying operating conditions. The second part happens on the degradation signal, i.e., the degradation signal will jump at the condition change-points. Different from existing literature, we define failure as the time when the system state reaches a predetermined failure threshold, rather than based on the amplitude of the degradation signal. It can effectively avoid the occurrence of false alarms, thus will have a positive impact on the performance of RUL prediction.

Specifically, we propose a two-factor state-space model based method (hereafter referred to as F2S2) for RUL prediction under timevarying operating conditions. The term "two-factor" refers to the two effects of time-varying operating conditions, i.e., changes in degradation rates and jumps in degradation signals. A state transition function that follows a Wiener process is used to describe the underlying degradation process of the system state. A time-scale transformation is applied to the Wiener process to capture the effects of time-varying degradation rates. A measurement function depending on the system state is used to describe the degradation signal. Jumps that occur in the degradation signal at condition change-points are smoothed using a transformation function that maps different operating conditions to a pre-specified baseline condition. The RUL is defined as the time when the underlying system state reaches a predetermined failure threshold. We assume that operating conditions are known and follow a predetermined profile. The major contributions of this work are summarized as follows.

- This first contribution of this paper focuses on the development of a state-space model framework for RUL prediction of partially degraded systems operating under time-varying conditions. This modeling framework improves upon existing models by reducing false alarms and improving prediction accuracy. The modeling framework considers two effects of the time-varying operating conditions, namely changes in degradation rates and jumps in degradation signals. Changes in degradation rates are considered in the state transition function, and the signal jumps are considered in the measurement function of our model. This kind of framework allows us to separate the unique relationship between degradation rates and changing conditions while minimizing the false alarms that result from signal jumps.
- The second contribution is related to the derivation of a closed-form solution of the RUL probability density function (PDF) under timevarying operating conditions. This is accomplished by transforming time-varying degradation rates into a fixed baseline degradation rate through time-scale transformation that is a function of the operating conditions.
- The third contribution revolves around a signal transformation algorithm that smoothens the signal jumps occurring at condition change-points. This transformation algorithm applies to the measurement function component of our state-space modeling framework, and is used to recalibrate abrupt signal jumps that causes false alarms and improves the accuracy of RUL prediction.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 introduces the framework of conventional RUL prediction methods based on state-space models. Section 3 proposes the F2S2 method to predict the RUL under time-varying operating conditions. In Section 4, the effectiveness of the proposed method is demonstrated using a simulation study. In Section 5, the performance of the proposed method is verified by an experimental dataset of rolling element bearings. Section 6 provides some conclusions and directions for future research.

## 2. Conventional RUL prediction framework based on state-space models

State-space models have been applied into the RUL prediction in some literature [20–24]. These conventional prognostic methodologies did not take into account the effects of time-varying operating conditions. They assumed that the operating conditions remain unchanged during the whole lifetime. However, state-space models provide a theoretical framework to analyze the behaviors of the inherent system state and the degradation signal in parallel, which is suitable for dealing with the issue of time-varying operating conditions. Therefore, in this section, we provide an overview for the basic theories of conventional RUL prediction methodologies based on state-space models. These basic theories will provide fundamental knowledge for our framework.

## 2.1. State-space model

In real applications, degradation processes are often difficult to measure directly. Consequently, our underlying assumption is that the degradation signal of a system can be used as a proxy of the hidden physical degradation process. Our framework is based on a state-space model, whereby a state transition function is used to express the underlying degradation process of the system state, and a measurement function is used to describe the relationship between the system state and the degradation signal. The first-principles associated with physical degradation, however, are hard to understand in many applications. In these cases, state-space models can be developed based on some data-driven empirical models, such as Wiener process models [21] and gamma process models [26]. In this study, we use a common Wiener process model [8, 21] as the basis of our modeling framework.

## 3. Proposed two-factor state-space model (F2S2) based RUL prediction method

State-space models separately express the inherent system state and the degradation signal using two different functions. They have the potential to distinguish the underlying physical degradation process and the dynamic behavior of the degradation signal. Therefore, in this section, we take advantage of this property and propose a F2S2 method for RUL prediction under time-varying operating conditions. The flowchart of the F2S2 method is shown in Fig. 2, which includes four steps: (1) state-space model development, (2) model parameter estimation, (3) system state estimation, and (4) RUL prediction. The detailed process of each step is explained in the following subsections.

## 5. Experimental demonstration

## 5.1. Experimental data introduction

In this section, a dataset acquired from accelerated degradation tests of rolling element thrust bearings [19] is used to demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method. The degradation tests were conducted under two different rotational speeds: 2200 rpm and 2600 rpm. An accelerometer was installed in the experimental setup to capture vibration signals of the bearings. Based on the industrial standards for machinery vibration ISO 2372, the experiment was stopped once the RMS of the vibration signals exceeds 2.2 G (G denotes gravitational acceleration). The average amplitude of the defective frequency and its first five harmonics was calculated to reflect the degradation of the bearings [18]. The degradation signals and condition profiles of four bearings are shown in Fig. 7. Bearings 1 and 2 operated under constant rotational speeds 2200 rpm and 2600 rpm, respectively. Bearings 3 and 4 operated under different time-varying speed profiles. Changes of rotational speeds cause obvious fluctuations in their signal amplitudes. To reduce the jumps caused by time-varying rotational speeds, the degradation signals are transformed into the baseline condition 2200 rpm by using the signal transformation algorithm in 3.2.2. The transformed signals are further smoothed to reduce the influence of random noise. The failure time of bearings is determined as the time when the smoothed signal curve crosses a failure threshold. To make sure that all bearings have failed before the end of the experiment, we pre-specify the failure threshold as the minimum smoothed signal amplitude of the four bearings at their last sampling points, i.e., 0.0617 Vrms. At the beginning of the lifetime, the signal fluctuates randomly without showing any degradation information. This stage is useless for RUL prediction. As time goes on, the degradation signals present obvious increasing trends, which implies that the bearings have degraded gradually. The initial time of degradation is selected as the first predicting time (FPT) and the RUL is predicted from this time point.

## 6. Conclusions

Time-varying operating conditions bring challenges to RUL prediction of engineering systems. First, they change the degradation rate of systems, thus increasing the difficulty of degradation modeling. Second, they cause abrupt signal jumps at condition change-points, leading to false alarms and poor prediction accuracy. Aiming at these two major technical challenges, this paper proposed a F2S2 method to predict the RUL under time-varying operating conditions. The major innovation of the modeling framework is that it explicates the degradation process under time-varying operating conditions relying on a clear distinction between the dynamic behavior of the degradation signal and the underlying physical degradation process. Specifically, the underlying physical degradation process is modeled using a state transition function, whereby the change of degradation rates caused by time-varying operating conditions is taken into account. The degradation signal is expressed as a measurement function depending on the system state. Jumps in degradation signals are eliminated by transforming signals under different conditions into a pre-specified baseline condition. The separate analysis of the two factors makes it possible to distinguish their own contributions to RUL prediction, thus avoiding the occurrence of false alarms and improving the prediction accuracy.

The technical contributions of this work are as follows. 1) A statespace model framework is developed for RUL prediction of systems operating under time-varying conditions. Unlike conventional datadriven prognostic models, this modeling framework provides a clear distinction between the dynamic behavior of the degradation signal and the underlying physical degradation process. This distinction helps prevent false alarms generated by signal jumps in condition changepoints. 2) A closed-form solution of the RUL distribution under timevarying operating conditions is derived based on a time-scale transformed Wiener process by considering the change of degradation rates. 3) A signal transformation algorithm is proposed to transform the degradation signals under different operating conditions into a baseline condition, which helps recalibrate abrupt signal jumps that causes false alarms and improves the accuracy of RUL prediction. A simulation study and an experimental study were conducted to demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method. The results verified the effectiveness of the proposed method in RUL prediction under time-varying operating conditions.

There are still some limitations in the proposed method. 1) Future condition profiles were assumed to be predetermined in this study. However, future condition profiles are unavailable in some industrial cases. Hence, how to predict the RUL under randomly evolving operating conditions will be researched in the future study. 2) Our modeling framework is only applicable for cases where the condition profile is piecewise constant.
